Verbal Words

111 words to know before the Verbal Retry

These are the words from the Verbal practice test you took in September: the question words, the right answers, the answers that tempted you, and similar words worth learning with them.

How to use this list
  1. Read each word, its meaning and the example.
  2. Press Cover meanings. Say the meaning of each word, then tap the box to check.
  3. When you know every word, open the Verbal Retry in HM.

New words to learn first

Words that were new. Learn the meaning first; guessing from how a word looks (android, metallic) leads to the wrong answer.

boughn.

a large main branch of a tree

Snow weighed down the lowest bough of the pine.

limbn.

a large branch of a tree; also an arm or leg

A broken limb blocked the garden path after the storm.

foliagen.

the leaves of a plant or tree

The autumn foliage turned the hills red and gold.

corpulentadj.

(of a person) very fat

The corpulent innkeeper squeezed between the crowded tables.

obeseadj.

extremely overweight

The vet warned that the cat was becoming obese.

excessiveadj.

more than is needed or reasonable

Excessive homework left no time for sleep.

stoutadj.

rather fat or heavily built

A stout farmer lifted the bale with ease.

rotundadj.

round and plump

The rotund little teapot sat on the windowsill.

androgynousadj.

having both male and female characteristics; not clearly masculine or feminine

The designer's androgynous jackets were made to suit anyone.

unisexadj.

designed for both men and women

The shop sells unisex sweaters in every size.

Close, but not the same meaning

You knew the area of meaning but picked a related word: an action instead of a quality, an example instead of the general word, or something linked to it.

lenientadj.

not strict; mild or merciful when judging or punishing

The lenient referee gave the player only a warning.

compassionateadj.

feeling or showing sympathy for someone who is suffering

A compassionate nurse stayed late to comfort the frightened boy.

clementadj.

merciful in judging; also (of weather) mild

The clement judge reduced the sentence to community service.

permissiveadj.

allowing a lot of freedom; not strict about rules

Permissive rules let the campers stay up as late as they liked.

laxadj.

not strict or careful enough

Lax security let the visitors wander into the storeroom.

burnishv.

to polish metal or wood by rubbing until it shines

The craftsman burnished the brass handle to a warm glow.

buffv.

to polish with a soft cloth

He buffed his shoes until he could see his reflection.

furbishv.

to polish or brighten something up; to renovate

The volunteers furbished the old gate before the festival.

eran.

a long period of history with a particular character

The first moon landing began a new era of space travel.

epochn.

a period of time marked by important events or changes

The printing press opened an epoch of cheaper books.

historicadj.

famous or important in history

The treaty was signed on a historic afternoon in 1783.

antiquityn.

the ancient past, especially before the Middle Ages

The museum displays coins from antiquity.

historicaladj.

connected with the past or the study of history (compare historic: important)

The library keeps historical records of every flood.

discreetadj.

careful and tactful in what one says or does, especially about private matters

A discreet friend never repeats what you tell her.

tactn.

skill in dealing with people without upsetting them

With great tact, the coach suggested a different position.

withdrawnadj.

quiet and shy; avoiding other people

After the move, he grew withdrawn and rarely spoke in class.

pragmaticadj.

practical; focused on what will actually work

Her pragmatic plan used the money the club already had.

discreteadj.

separate and distinct (a look-alike of discreet)

The course is divided into six discrete units.

diplomaticadj.

handling people politely and carefully, without causing bad feelings

Her diplomatic reply calmed both angry neighbors.

intersectv.

to cross or meet at a point

The two hiking trails intersect near the old bridge.

convergev.

to come together from different directions and meet

Fans converged on the square after the final.

bypassv.

to go around something instead of through it

The new highway bypasses the town center.

divergev.

to separate and go in different directions (opposite of converge)

The paths diverge at the edge of the forest.

punyadj.

small and weak

The puny seedling bent in the slightest wind.

sicklyadj.

often ill; looking unhealthy

The sickly child missed many days of school.

frailadj.

physically weak and easily hurt

The frail bird could barely lift its wings.

scrawnyadj.

unattractively thin and small

A scrawny cat waited by the back door for scraps.

marv.

to spoil or damage the look or quality of something

One rude remark marred an otherwise happy party.

tarnishv.

to make a metal dull; to damage a good name

The scandal tarnished the senator's reputation.

blemishn./v.

a mark or flaw; to spoil with a flaw

A single blemish on the painting lowered its price.

taintv.

to spoil by adding something bad

Rumors of cheating tainted the team's victory.

A second meaning or another word class

The headword is used in a less common sense or as a different part of speech. Check the word class of the options.

maneuvern.

a clever, planned move made to gain an advantage

Moving the meeting to Friday was a maneuver to keep the rival team away.

contrivancen.

a clever plan or scheme, often a tricky one; also a clever device

The fake errand was a contrivance to get him out of the house before the party.

deviatev.

to turn away from a usual course or plan

The pilot deviated from the route to avoid the storm.

stratagemn.

a clever trick used to gain an advantage

The general's stratagem lured the enemy into the valley.

gambitn.

an opening move made to gain an advantage

Offering to wash the car was her gambit to borrow it on Saturday.

nulladj.

amounting to nothing; having zero value (also: having no legal force)

The test showed a null effect: the new fertilizer changed nothing.

unjustifiedadj.

not supported by good reasons

The fans' anger at the referee was unjustified; the call was correct.

voidadj.

having no legal force; empty

The ticket is void if the date has been changed.

niln.

nothing; zero

The visitors' chances of winning were close to nil.

founderv.

(of a ship) to fill with water and sink; (of a plan) to fail

The fishing boat foundered in the storm.

locatorn.

a person or device that finds the position of something

The phone's locator showed the lost bag at the station.

capsizev.

(of a boat) to turn over in the water

A sudden wave capsized the canoe.

falterv.

to lose strength or confidence and begin to fail

The project faltered when the money ran out.

Strength and tone

The answer must keep the full strength and tone of the headword, not a weaker or looser cousin.

replican.

an exact copy, often a model

The gift shop sells a small replica of the Liberty Bell.

facsimilen.

an exact copy, especially of a document or picture

Visitors may handle a facsimile of the original map.

decoyn.

something used to lure or mislead, such as a fake bird that attracts real ones

Hunters set a wooden duck on the pond as a decoy.

reproductionn.

a copy of a work of art, made openly

A reproduction of the painting hangs in the hallway.

singularadj.

one of a kind; remarkable (not the grammar sense 'not plural')

She showed singular courage during the rescue.

matchlessadj.

so good that nothing can equal it

The pianist played with matchless skill.

unrivaledadj.

better than all others

The museum has an unrivaled collection of fossils.

repudiatev.

to reject or disown something openly

The senator repudiated the false quote printed under her name.

renouncev.

to give up or reject something formally

He renounced his claim to the family farm.

recantv.

to take back a statement or belief publicly

The witness later recanted her story.

astuteadj.

quick to notice what matters and use it; sharp in judgment

An astute shopper spotted the pricing mistake at once.

shrewdadj.

clever in practical matters and hard to fool

The shrewd trader waited until prices fell before buying.

cannyadj.

careful and clever, especially with money or business

A canny shopper waited for the January sales.

Analogy words: same or opposite

Pairs of words that mean the same or the opposite, plus their look-alikes.

contiguousadj.

touching or sharing a border; next to each other

The forty-eight contiguous states share borders with their neighbors.

adjacentadj.

next to or near something

The library is adjacent to the gym.

continuousadj.

without a break in time or space (a look-alike of contiguous)

The machine makes a continuous humming noise.

assuredadj.

confident and sure of oneself

She gave an assured answer without looking at her notes.

vigilantadj.

carefully watching for danger or problems

Vigilant lifeguards scanned the waves all afternoon.

obstinateadj.

stubbornly refusing to change one's mind

The obstinate mule would not cross the stream.

waryadj.

careful because of possible danger

The deer stayed wary of the hikers.

integratev.

to combine parts into a whole

The new students were quickly integrated into the class.

coalescev.

to come together to form one mass or group

Small puddles coalesced into one wide pool.

segregatev.

to separate one group from others

The recycling plant segregates glass from plastic.

isolatev.

to set apart from others

Doctors isolated the patient to stop the infection spreading.

dispersev.

to scatter in different directions

The crowd dispersed after the concert.

amalgamatev.

to join two or more things into one

The two small schools amalgamated last year.

splicev.

to join two pieces of rope, film or wire end to end

The editor spliced two scenes together.

fusionn.

the joining of two or more things into one

The band's music is a fusion of jazz and folk.

fissionn.

the splitting of something into two or more parts

Some single-celled organisms reproduce by fission, splitting into two.

fissuren.

a long, narrow crack or opening

Steam rose from a fissure in the volcanic rock.

riftn.

a crack or split; also a break in a friendship

A rift opened between the two old friends.

severv.

to cut off or cut apart

The storm severed the power line.

Analogy words: stronger feelings

Read across the pairs: each word is a much stronger form of the one before.

ecstaticadj.

extremely happy

The team was ecstatic after the last-second goal.

panickedadj.

suddenly so frightened that one cannot think clearly

A panicked driver swerved off the icy road.

elatedadj.

very happy and excited

She was elated to hear she had won the scholarship.

franticadj.

wild with fear or worry

Frantic parents searched the park for the missing boy.

Analogy words: sources, products and places

Where a material comes from, and what is made from it.

drabadj.

dull and without color

The drab gray walls made the room feel cold.

flaxn.

a plant whose stem fibers are spun into linen thread

Farmers soaked the flax stems to loosen the fibers.

linenn.

cloth woven from flax fibers

Linen shirts stay cool in hot weather.

oren.

rock from which a metal can be extracted

The ore was crushed and heated to remove the iron.

textilen.

any woven or knitted cloth

The town grew rich from its textile mills.

graniten.

a very hard, speckled rock used for building

The kitchen counter is made of polished granite.

quarryn.

a large open pit where stone is cut or dug out

Workers cut marble blocks from the quarry.

extractv.

to remove or take out, often with effort

Miners extract copper from the ore.

Analogy words: people and their roles

Who holds a position, who wants it, and what each person does.

aspirantn.

a person who hopes to gain a position or honor

Every aspirant to the team attended the summer tryout.

incumbentn.

the person who currently holds an office

The incumbent won a second term as mayor.

alumnusn.

a former student of a school or college (plural: alumni)

An alumnus of the school returned to give the graduation speech.

predecessorn.

the person who held a job before the current holder

The new principal kept many of her predecessor's rules.

successorn.

the person who takes a job after someone else

The retiring captain trained his successor for a year.

Analogy words: measuring and damaging

Measurements of shapes, and words for harming a good name.

sulliedadj.

stained; made less pure or less respected

Years of rumors left the coach's good name sullied.

smearedv. (past)

damaged someone's reputation with false claims; also spread something greasy

The article smeared the mayor with invented charges.

terminatedv. (past)

ended, especially a job or contract

Her contract was terminated after the merger.

defamev.

to damage someone's good name with false statements

The actor sued the magazine for defaming him.

besmirchv.

to damage a reputation; literally, to make dirty

He refused to besmirch his rival's name to win votes.

circumferencen.

the distance around a circle

We measured the circumference of the tree trunk with a tape.

perimetern.

the distance around the edge of a flat shape

The perimeter of the square garden is forty feet.

polygonn.

a flat shape with three or more straight sides

A hexagon is a polygon with six sides.
