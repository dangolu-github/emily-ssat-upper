Detail and evidence questions: check all five

  • Question words: According to the passage… · The passage gives evidence to answer which question? These ask about one line, not the whole structure.
  • Two-way check: eliminate (cross out what the passage does not support) and confirm (find the line that supports the answer). Check each option against the passage, one by one.
  • Match exactly: the time (since the early 1900s ≠ before the nineteenth century), the actor, and the certainty (will likely ≠ will; agreed that cuts are required ≠ are taking steps).
  • Use the passage, not your own knowledge: a rise in air and sea temperature is one effect; it does not say when global warming began.
  • Easy options go fast; the trap is usually the one that sounds like the passage’s topic (lasting consequences).

Main idea in a history passage: read the skeleton

  • Skeleton sentences: the first and last sentence of each paragraph. Read them first.
  • Follow the logic chain, then skip the detail lines (harassed, threatened and jailed).
True Levellers form (goal: share the land)→renamed the Diggers; communities flourish→landowners push back; groups disbanded
  • Background ≠ main idea: great social unrest is the reason the group existed. The answer must cover the whole chain: the brief history of an English community organization.
  • Repeated capitalized names (True Levellers, Diggers) mark the subject.

Tone of a poem: who is speaking, and what do they want?

  • Imperatives give orders or requests and start with the verb: Up! up! · quit your books · Come, hear · Come forth · Let Nature be your teacher.
  • Count the word types: the stanzas are full of verbs, with very few words of delight. The pleasant images (first sweet evening yellow) are there to persuade the friend to go outside.
  • Decide from the last stanza: the earlier stanzas prepare for it. Here it ends with a plea.
  • imploring = begging earnestly. blissful = full of joy, the speaker’s own feeling.

2. Reading: main idea · Pluto

The main idea covers the whole passage, and only this passage

  • Give each paragraph a job first, then find the option that every job fits.
  • Too broad: true of the field, not this text (Many mysteries remain in the field of astronomy).
  • Not stated: an opinion the author never gives (It is crucial that we reach a consensus).
  • Signal words: indicating, suggesting introduce a conclusion; dates (1930, the late 1970s, 2006) show a timeline.

Distinctions from class

  • replicate / duplicate: duplicate = make an exact copy of an object; replicate = repeat a process, result or experiment.
  • negligible / negligent / neglect: too small to matter · careless · fail to give care or attention.
  • liability / reliable: different words. A liability works against you.
  • stem / stem from: stem + a flow = halt it; stem from = come from, originate in. The options decide which sense is tested (S6: halt, not originate).
  • exploit can be neutral (the birds exploit the wind) or negative (exploit workers or enslaved people). Its synonyms: utilize, harness, make use of, capitalize on, take advantage of.
  • prevailing is good in formal essays instead of popular. Synonyms: dominant, predominant, prevalent, widespread; opposite: rare, minority.

Synonym checks: sustain = maintain · grueling = exhausting (not hazardous, which means dangerous) · exploit = utilize · prevailing = dominant · negligible = insignificant (enormous is the opposite) · stem = halt.

4. Analogies: “a kind of”

The category connector

  • Connector sentence: ___ is a kind of ___. A tern is a kind of bird. A beagle is a kind of hound.
  • Keep the direction: reptile : lizard is reversed: a lizard is a kind of reptile.
  • Use the smallest connector: a general sentence lets too many options pass. A nest is part of a tree is loose; the precise relation is a nest is an object built on a tree, so nest : tree is not “a kind of”.
  • One step only: beagle → hound, not beagle → animal.
PairRelationship
lens : binocularspart–whole

primate : mammal :: conifer : treea kind of (a pyramid is a kind of monument)
insight : understanding :: glimpse : sighta kind of: a sudden, deep understanding · a quick, brief look

Category words

  • hound: a dog used for hunting by scent; poems often say hound for dog.
  • primate: the group of monkeys, apes and humans (not “the top of the food chain”).
  • conifer: a tree that bears cones: pine, fir, spruce.
  • arachnid: spiders and scorpions, a group beside insects, not inside them.
