Synonyms · Group 01

Setting out

Word meanings

exhilarating

adj.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

Making one feel very happy, lively and energetic; thrilling.

After six months behind a counter, the first morning on the open road was exhilarating: the clerk, who had expected to feel only fear, found himself laughing into the wind.

emancipation

n.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

The act of setting free from control, restriction or slavery; the freedom that results.

Leaving the city felt less like a journey than an emancipation, as though every rule of the counting-house had been left behind at the river.

vivacious

adj.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

Lively, spirited and full of energy (of a person or manner).

The driver's wife was so vivacious, singing over the rattle of the wheels, that the silent passengers seemed half asleep beside her.

expanse

n.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

A wide, open and continuous area (of land, sea or sky).

Beyond the last fence lay an expanse of grass so unbroken that the eye, used to streets and walls, could find nothing to rest on.

arduous

adj.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

Demanding great effort and endurance; very hard to do or to travel.

The crossing was arduous rather than dangerous: no one was hurt, but every mile of the climb had to be paid for in sweat.

Answer Dthrilling

Show explanationHide explanation
  • Meaning / prediction: making you feel thrilled and full of life.
  • Form: adjective (from the verb exhilarate).
  • Nuance: a strong, energetic kind of happiness, not a mild or quiet one.

Each option

  • ATedious is the opposite: dull and tiring.
  • BAmusing is pleasant, but mild and about humour; it sits far lower on the scale.
  • CHazardous describes danger on a journey, not the feeling it gives.
  • DThrilling = producing excitement and delight: match.
  • EAncient concerns age.

Synonyms · Group 02

Hard country

Word meanings

desolate

adj.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

Empty of people and bleak; (of a person) utterly lonely and unhappy.

The valley was not barren, for grass grew thickly along the creek, yet it was desolate: no smoke rose, no fence stood, and no one had passed that way in months.

hardy

adj.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

Able to endure difficult conditions; robust.

Only the hardy oxen kept their pace through the early frost; the horses, though faster, had to be fed from the wagons' dwindling grain.

foolhardy

adj.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

Recklessly bold; taking risks without thinking (not the same as brave, and not the same as hardy).

Crossing the swollen river at dusk was not brave but foolhardy, since the same ford would have been safe by morning.

dearth

n.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

A scarcity or lack of something (the opposite of abundant supply).

Where the guidebook had promised abundant pasture, the party found a dearth of grass, and the animals grew thin within a week.

relinquish

v.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

To give up or let go of (a claim, a possession, a hold), often reluctantly.

One by one the families relinquished the heavy things they had sworn to keep: a stove, a looking-glass, and at last a chest of books.

FOOLHARDY:

FOOLHARDY (adj.)Synonyms: reckless, rash, unwise, imprudent, daredevil, heedless
Antonyms: cautious, prudent, careful, sensible
Related: hardy (tough, enduring) is inside the word but not inside the meaning; brave takes a risk that is worth taking, foolhardy takes one that is not
  1. Aunbelievable
  2. Bintolerant
  3. Cambiguous
  4. Dimpatient
  5. Eunwise✓ correct answer
Show answerHide answer

Answer Eunwise

Show explanationHide explanation
  • Meaning / prediction: recklessly bold; taking a risk without thinking.
  • Form: adjective; fool + hardy, so the boldness is foolish.
  • Nuance: a judgement that the risk was a bad idea, so the synonym must carry that criticism.

Each option

  • AUnbelievable concerns whether something can be believed.
  • BIntolerant describes refusing to accept other people or views.
  • CAmbiguous means having more than one possible meaning.
  • DImpatient is restless about waiting; a foolhardy act may be sudden, but the word judges the risk, not the waiting.
  • EUnwise = showing poor judgement: the criticism inside foolhardy. Match.

DESOLATE (adj.)Synonyms: place: deserted, bleak, barren, uninhabited, forsaken; person: forlorn, wretched, inconsolable
Antonyms: bustling, populous, thriving, lively; (person) cheerful, comforted
Related: desolation (n.), desolate (v., to lay waste); the word has a place sense and a person sense, and the options decide which
  1. Aarid
  2. Bsilent
  3. Cdeserted✓ correct answer
  4. Dbustling
  5. Esudden
Show answerHide answer

Answer Cdeserted

Show explanationHide explanation
  • Meaning / prediction: empty of people and bleak.
  • Form: adjective with two senses (a desolate plain; a desolate widow); the options are all about places, so the place sense is wanted.
  • Nuance: emptiness and bleakness, not dryness, not quietness.

Each option

  • AArid means dry; a well-watered valley can still be desolate if no one lives there.
  • BSilent is often true of a desolate place, but a crowded library is silent too; an effect, not the meaning.
  • CDeserted = empty of people, abandoned: match.
  • DBustling is the opposite.
  • ESudden concerns time.

Synonyms · Group 03

Naming the mood

Word meanings

apprehensive

adj.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

Anxious or uneasy about something that may happen (milder than terrified).

She was not yet afraid of the mountains, only apprehensive: the guides' silence about the pass troubled her more than any warning would have.

despondent

adj.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

Having lost hope; deeply discouraged (stronger than disappointed; dejected lies between).

After the third wagon broke an axle, the men were no longer merely disappointed but despondent, sitting by the fire without speaking of tomorrow.

exuberant

adj.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

Overflowing with high spirits and energy (stronger than cheerful).

The children were exuberant at the first sight of the river, racing along the bank, while the adults, merely relieved, checked the depth of the ford.

placid

adj.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

Calm and untroubled (of a person, a mood or water); not easily excited.

The river that had roared all night was placid by dawn, and the captain, as unhurried as the water, gave the order to cross.

nostalgic

adj.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

Feeling a sentimental longing for a past time or place.

The letter home began bravely but turned nostalgic by the second page, lingering over the smell of the bakery she had been so eager to leave.
