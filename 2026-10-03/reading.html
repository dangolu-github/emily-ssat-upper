Passage 1

The Arctic tern undertakes the longest migration of any animal on Earth, traveling roughly 44,000 miles each year between its breeding grounds in the Arctic and its wintering areas near Antarctica. Scientists long marveled at this feat but struggled to understand how a bird weighing barely four ounces could sustain such an extraordinary journey. Recent research using miniature tracking devices has revealed that Arctic terns do not fly in a straight line; instead, they follow a winding, S-shaped route that takes advantage of prevailing wind patterns. By riding these winds, the terns expend far less energy than they would on a direct path. The study also found that terns spend surprisingly little time resting during migration, often flying for days without stopping. One of the researchers noted that the terns' route “shows a remarkable ability to exploit environmental conditions—these birds have essentially mapped the atmosphere.” Despite the grueling distance, Arctic terns can live for over thirty years, suggesting that their migration strategy, far from being a liability, may actually contribute to their longevity by keeping them in regions of abundant food year-round.

A Observed result, measurement, explanation

Observed result, measurement, explanation. A scientific passage usually contains three different kinds of statement:

  • Observed result: what was seen or found (the terns follow an S-shaped route).
  • Measurement or method: how it was found (miniature tracking devices).
  • Proposed explanation: why it happens (riding the winds saves energy).
  • What questions test: an observed fact is not the same as the reason offered for it, and a reason offered is not the same as a proven cause.

B Precise details and the limits of an inference

Precise details and the limits of an inference.

  • Detail answer: a paraphrase of one sentence, not a copy of its words.
  • Wrong answers fail in four ways:
    • Wrong part: a true detail taken from another part of the passage.
    • Outside: information the passage never states.
    • Twisted: the relationship between the passage's own words is changed.
    • Too strong: the claim is stronger than the passage made it.
  • Inference: one small step beyond what is stated.
  • Limit: keep the passage's qualifiers (may, suggests) and reject any option that needs a second, unsupported step.

  1. A straight line from the Arctic to Antarctica
  2. A random, unpredictable path that varies each year
  3. A curved path designed to take advantage of wind patterns
  4. A short coastal route that avoids open ocean
  5. A route that follows the migration of other bird species

Answer

Answer: C. “A winding, S-shaped route that takes advantage of prevailing wind patterns.” A contradicts the passage; B, D and E add things the passage never says.

  3. “a remarkable ability to exploit environmental conditions”

Answer

Answer: C. Ability and exploit describe purposeful use of the winds. The other phrases give distance, weight, endurance and lifespan, none of which shows intention.

Practice

R5. The researcher’s statement that the terns “have essentially mapped the atmosphere” uses figurative language. Based on the context of the entire passage, which of the following best captures what this metaphor implies?

  1. The terns have a visual map of the Earth stored in their memory.
  2. The terns possess an instinctive understanding of atmospheric wind patterns that allows them to navigate efficiently.
  3. The terns use magnetic fields in the atmosphere to find their way.
  4. Scientists have created maps showing where terns fly, and the terns follow those maps.
  5. The terns communicate wind pattern information to each other during flight.
Show answerHide answer

Answer

Answer: B. The passage is about using wind patterns; mapped the atmosphere means the birds behave as if they had charted the winds. A takes mapped literally; C, D and E bring in ideas (magnetism, human maps, communication) that the passage never mentions.
