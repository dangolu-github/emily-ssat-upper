1. Homework review: three questions

18 / 21 · Analogies 8 / 10 · Reading 10 / 11.

QuestionYour choiceCorrect answerKey point
Q1 · Scrutinize : examineC · frequent : attendA · analyze : considerSame action, one level up. To scrutinize is to examine closely; to analyze is to consider closely.
Q2 · Hurt : debilitatedD · exuberant : despondentC · happy : ecstaticSame direction, much stronger. Exuberant and despondent are opposites, not two strengths of one feeling.
Q21 · Mood of lines 14–17C · mellowD · broodingDusk, mist, lonely, dim make the lines dark and heavy. Mellow is calm and pleasant; it is not related to melancholy.

Find the exact connector

  • Same meaning is the easy type and is rare in analogies. Same context is not a relationship at all.
  • melody : harmony · both belong to music, and neither is a kind of the other. Melody is the main tune; harmony is the other notes sung with it.
  • frequent · usually an adjective, happening often. As a verb it means to visit a place often: she frequents the café. That sense has nothing to do with attend.
  • squint : perceive · to squint is a physical action. To perceive is to notice and understand.
  • detain · (1) to delay, to hold someone back: the more frequent meaning; (2) to hold someone officially, as the police do: the harder meaning on the test.
  • fatigued : tired · runs stronger to weaker. The first pair runs weaker to stronger, so the order is wrong.

Words from the options

  • satiated · completely full and satisfied. Compare saturated, holding as much as it can: the sponge is saturated with water; the color is highly saturated.
  • solemn · serious and formal, as at an important ceremony; not gloomy. frail = weak, so frail : solemn has no link.
  • exuberant · full of joy and energy, a level above excited, the same level as ecstatic. despondent · discouraged, without hope.
  • indifferent · not caring, no interest, no feeling; it can describe a person’s whole manner. Not “very different”. In older writing it can also mean ordinary, not special.

Reading a poem’s mood

  • Read the first line of each stanza: is this scenery, or a scene used to think about something?
  • Go to the lines the question names and find the hint words: dusk (after sunset), mist, lonely, dim.
  • Remove what cannot fit (joyous, violent, embarrassed), then decide between what is left with the hint words. If you can remove at least two, make a guess.
  • Seasons set the mood. Spring and summer: vivid, energetic. Autumn: calm, peaceful, fading (losing brightness), nostalgic, melancholy, reflective. Winter: a stronger sadness.
  • Reflective poems use a scene to think about something serious, as in Robert Frost’s “The Road Not Taken”: two roads in an autumn wood.

2. Fifteen words: perspective, appearance and reality

These words are common answer choices in tone questions, and common distractors. Ask what each one describes.

How a narrator sees · the overall tone

WordMeaningKeep apart
candid adj.Honest and direct, even when it is awkward.
  • Usually praise.
  • blunt: very direct, a little too direct.
  • tactless: careless of other people’s feelings. Negative.
cynical adj.Doubting that anyone is sincere; disappointed with the world and mocking about it.
  • Always negative; not “clever”.
  • Common in speeches and in descriptions of a person’s outlook: Years of broken promises had left the villagers cynical.
ingenuous adj.Innocent and trusting.
  • Positive or neutral.
  • naive: the same innocence, said as a fault.
  • Not ingenious (clever).
dispassionate adj.Not swayed by strong feeling, so able to judge fairly.
  • More than calm.
  • impartial: fair to both sides: an impartial judge.
  • indifferent: does not care at all.
wry adj.Dryly funny, ironic, a little mocking.
  • Synonyms: ironic, sarcastic.
  • Not sneaky, and not simply funny: “A fine day for a picnic,” she said with a wry glance at the rain.

Judging and holding back · a final attitude

WordMeaningKeep apart
ambivalent adj.Having two opposite feelings about something at the same time.
  • ambi- = both, two.
  • Not hesitant, which does not suggest two feelings.
  • dilemma (n.): stuck between two hard choices. Related, but a noun, so never its synonym.

  • Same family as indifferent: both show little feeling toward others.

  • Not related to complain.
  • On the test it is negative: After three easy victories the team grew complacent.

  • In religion: devout, pious (deeply religious).
  • zealous: extremely eager, often too eager; a frequent distractor.
  • Not jealous.
disdain n., v.The feeling that someone or something is beneath you; to look down on.
  • Strongly negative.
  • Different from discern, though they look alike.

Also from class

  • deceptive · dishonest: hiding the truth or creating a false impression, as in a deceptive advertisement. Stronger than sneaky.

3. Reading: a tone question

Q1. The writer’s reaction when looking out at the lake is one of … A · wonder.

How to answer

  1. Find the moment in the question: looking out at the lake. The passage has two: the sun rising, then the moment they turn and face the lake.
  2. Find the words that carry feeling. Sparkled, bounced, shimmering describe the scene; they show no strong feeling. Our breath was taken away does.
  3. Give each option a side: wonder (strong positive), amusement (strong positive), melancholy (negative), understanding (neutral), unresponsiveness (no reaction).
  4. Check the meaning against the text.

Keep apart

  • wonder (n.) · amazement and admiration at something beautiful or remarkable. Not the verb, I wonder = I am curious.
  • amusement · something makes you want to laugh: a joke, a comic story. Alexander Pope’s comic poem about a stolen lock of hair is amused; a sunrise over a lake is not.
  • A lively scene does not make the writer amused or happy. Look for the feeling itself.

4. Stronger and weaker: verbs and adjectives

Last class covered nouns (wind, rain, snow), the type that appears most often. Verbs are harder. These ladders sit behind Q1 and Q2.

Three rules

  • One dimension per ladder. Scrutinize is to examine as analyze is to consider: both pairs change how closely. Frequent is to attend changes how often: same shape, different dimension, wrong answer.
  • Same feeling, stronger feeling; never the opposite feeling. Hurt is to debilitated as happy is to ecstatic. Exuberant is to despondent is an opposite pair: two strong words are not a degree.
  • Say the dimension in the connector: “to ___ is to ___ more closely / more forcefully / more openly / more greedily”; then test every option with the same sentence.
