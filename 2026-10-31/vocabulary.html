Fifteen words: perspective, appearance and reality

Perspective: who looks, what shows, what is real

  • Three questions behind every narrator: who is looking, what do they show, and what is really there?
  • Appearance words name what is shown: facade, ostentatious, feign, specious. Reality words name what is found out: discern, candid.
  • Attitude words name how the looker judges: cynical, ingenuous, dispassionate, wry, complacent, fervent, ambivalent, aloof, disdain. These are the words the test uses in tone and narrator questions.
  • For each word: meaning, part of speech, sign (+ / − / none) and strength. The sentence shows the word doing its job; say what in the sentence proves the meaning.

 

How a narrator sees

candid

adj.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

Honest and direct: saying what one really thinks, even when it is awkward.

Sign · strength: + · 1

Asked whether the play had bored him, the critic was candid: he had enjoyed the costumes and very little else.

cynical

adj.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

Believing that people act only from self-interest, and doubting that anyone is sincere.

Sign · strength: − · 2

Years of broken promises had left the villagers cynical, so they shrugged at the mayor’s newest pledge to repair the bridge.

ingenuous

adj.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

Innocent and trusting; unable to suspect a trick (not the same as ingenious).

Sign · strength: + · 1 (innocent; naive is the same thing said as a fault)

It was ingenuous of the new clerk to assume that every customer who asked for a refund had actually bought the coat.

dispassionate

adj.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

Not swayed by feeling; judging calmly and fairly, as if the matter were not one’s own.

Sign · strength: 0 · no rung: it withholds feeling

The surveyor described the flooded farms in a dispassionate voice, listing acres and depths as though reading a timetable.

wry

adj.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

Dryly humorous, with a twist that shows the speaker knows things are not as they should be.

Sign · strength: + / − · 1 (a twist, not a blow)

“A fine day for a picnic,” she said with a wry glance at the rain streaming down the window.

Appearance and reality

facade

n.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

A false outward appearance that hides what is really there; originally, the front of a building.

Sign · strength: − · 1

Behind the family’s facade of calm, the servants knew, lay a quarrel that had lasted twenty years.

ostentatious

adj.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

Showy; meant to impress people with wealth, taste or importance.

Sign · strength: − · 2

The merchant’s ostentatious carriage, gilded even on its wheels, announced his arrival long before he spoke.

specious

adj.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

Seeming true or right on the surface, but false underneath.

Sign · strength: − · 2

The salesman’s argument was specious: it sounded like arithmetic and added up to nothing.

feign

v.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

To pretend to feel or have something one does not.

Sign · strength: − · 1

Roberta feigned interest in the lecture on drainage, nodding at intervals while she planned her escape.

discern

v.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

To see or notice something that is not obvious; to tell one thing from another.

Sign · strength: 0 · praises the looker, not the thing

Only a practiced eye could discern the faint repair that ran across the painting’s sky.

Judging and holding back

ambivalent

adj.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

Having two opposite feelings about something at the same time.

Sign · strength: + and − at once · 2

He was ambivalent about the move: proud of the new post, yet unwilling to leave the river he had grown up beside.

aloof

adj.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

Keeping apart from others; distant and uninvolved, by choice.

Sign · strength: − · 1

The new neighbor remained aloof all summer, lifting a hand to greet us but never crossing the lane.

complacent

adj.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

Pleased with oneself or one’s situation, and therefore blind to its dangers.

Sign · strength: − · 2

After three easy victories the team grew complacent, and the fourth match punished them for it.

fervent

adj.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

Showing strong, sincere feeling; intensely earnest.

Sign · strength: + · 2

The boy’s fervent promise to repay every cent silenced the shopkeeper’s lecture.

disdain

n., v.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

The feeling that someone or something is beneath you; to treat as unworthy of notice.

Sign · strength: − · 3

The duchess looked at the muddy boots with open disdain and did not ask the traveler to sit.
