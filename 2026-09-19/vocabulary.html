01 How believable is it?

plausible adj.

Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

Seeming reasonable or likely to be true.

Although the guide offered a plausible explanation for the missing inscription, no surviving document showed that the inscription had ever existed.

credible adj.

Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

Believable or worthy of trust.

The witness gave a credible account of the journey: several unfamiliar names she mentioned also appeared in records discovered years later.

credulous adj.

Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

Too ready to believe something without enough evidence.

The credulous collector accepted the dramatic story about the painting, even though the seller could produce no record of its previous owners.

authentic adj.

Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

Genuine; actually what it is claimed to be.

The letter was authentic, but its writer had not witnessed the battle, so the historian still questioned the accuracy of its description.

spurious adj.

Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

False or not genuine, despite appearing valid or real.

The apparently ancient map proved spurious when an examiner discovered that its paper contained a material introduced only in the twentieth century.

Synonyms: Belief and appearance

02 How strong is the evidence?

conjecture n.

Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

An explanation or judgment formed from incomplete evidence.

Until a dated record linked the two travelers, the proposed meeting between them remained conjecture rather than an established event.

substantiate v.

Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

To support a claim with evidence.

To substantiate her claim that the workshop employed local apprentices, the researcher cited wage records naming several young residents of the town.

Class annotation

substantial: a strong or significant chance

corroborate v.

Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

To provide additional evidence that supports a statement or account.

An independently written diary helped corroborate the captain’s account by describing the same damaged bridge and the same unexpected delay.

conclusive adj.

Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

Strong enough to settle the particular question being examined.

A clear photograph taken inside the completed building in 1948 was conclusive evidence against the claim that construction had not begun until 1950.

Class annotation

decisive: personality. make decisions quickly

tentative adj.

Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

Not final or certain; open to revision.

Because several pages of the notebook were missing, the scientist offered only a tentative explanation of how the earlier experiment had been conducted.

Class annotation

uncertain not final hesitant provisional

Synonyms: Claims and evidence

Worked reasoning

Meaning / prediction: An explanation or judgment formed from incomplete evidence. Form: n. Nuance: Conjecture may later be supported or disproved; it is not automatically a lie.

Test all five:

  • A. Supposition is a judgment made without complete evidence.
  • B. Testimony is an account offered as evidence, not necessarily an inference from limited evidence.
  • C. An allegation is an assertion, often of wrongdoing; it need not have been formed by conjecture.
  • D. A recollection is a remembered event.
  • E. An explanation may be thoroughly established; it lacks the intended uncertainty.

Answer: A.

03 How should we examine and respond?

skeptical adj.

Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

Doubtful about the truth or value of a claim.

The historian remained skeptical of the heroic account, not because she disliked its subject, but because its details changed with each retelling.

Class annotation

suspicious doubtful questionable

scrutinize v.

Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

To examine something very closely and carefully.

Rather than accept the neat summary, the investigator chose to scrutinize the original entries, comparing their dates, handwriting, and unexplained corrections.

Class annotation

scrutiny

impartial adj.

Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

Fair; not favoring one side over another.

An impartial reviewer considered both the evidence supporting the discovery and the objections raised by researchers who doubted the original report.

Class annotation

fair, neutral, unbiased, objective

refute v.

Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

To show, through reasons or evidence, that a claim is false or mistaken.

The earlier photograph helped refute the assertion that the statue had always stood in the square, for it clearly showed the statue beside the river.

repudiate v.

Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

To reject or disown something explicitly.

The explorer chose to repudiate the boast attributed to her, stating that she had never claimed to have completed the journey without assistance.

Synonyms: Judgment and response
