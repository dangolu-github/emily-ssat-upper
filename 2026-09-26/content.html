American Independence
and Government

From British colonies to an independent nation with a constitutional government.

From colonial rule to independence

Britain governed thirteen colonies in North America. Disputes over British authority and representation grew into armed conflict in 1775. In 1776, the Declaration of Independence explained the reasons for separation and asserted the colonies’ right to govern themselves. It announced a political break; it did not create today’s three-branch government.

Three documents, three different jobs

DocumentMilestoneMain function
Declaration of Independence1776
Adopted		Explains and announces separation from Britain; states political ideals and grievances.
Constitution1787
Signed		Sets out the federal government’s structure and powers. Ratification followed; signing was not the final approval.
Bill of Rights1791
Ratified		The first ten amendments; protects liberties such as speech and religion and limits government power.

Before the Constitution, the Articles of Confederation gave the national government limited power. The new framework strengthened national authority while dividing and limiting it.

How power is organised

Legislative

Congress makes federal laws. It includes the Senate and House of Representatives.

Executive

The President heads the branch that carries out and enforces federal laws.

Judicial

Federal courts interpret laws and decide cases under the Constitution.

Separation of powers: different branches have different jobs. Checks and balances: each branch can limit others. Federalism: national and state governments have distinct, sometimes overlapping responsibilities.

Government responsibilities in the early republic

Read the passage and choose the best answer to each question.

1While with reverence and resignation we contemplate the dispensations of Divine
2Providence in the alarming and destructive pestilence with which several of our cities
3and towns have been visited, there is cause for gratitude and mutual congratulations
4that the malady has disappeared and that we are again permitted to assemble in safety
5at the seat of Government for the discharge of our important duties. But when we
6reflect that this fatal disorder has within a few years made repeated ravages in some
7of our principal sea ports, and with increased malignancy, and when we consider the
8magnitude of the evils arising from the interruption of public and private business,
9whereby the national interests are deeply affected, I think it my duty to invite the
10Legislature of the Union to examine the expediency of establishing suitable regulations
11in aid of the health laws of the respective States; for these being formed on the idea
12that contagious sickness may be communicated through the channels of commerce,
13there seems to be a necessity that Congress, who alone can regulate trade, should
14frame a system which, while it may tend to preserve the general health, may be
15compatible with the interests of commerce and the safety of the revenue.

1. The “ravages in some of our principal seaports” (lines 6-7) refers to

  • (A) losses during a battle
  • (B) damage from a hurricane
  • (C) spoilage of food to be shipped
  • (D) disease affecting people in business
  • (E) work stoppages relating to a bitter strike
Show answerHide answer

D · Meaning of a phrase

The ravages are damage caused by disease. D is the only choice that keeps the disease and business context together.

Evidence: The opening names a “pestilence” and a “malady”; “this fatal disorder” continues the same reference. The passage then describes its interruption of public and private business.

2. It can be inferred from the passage that Congress

  • (A) has failed in its duties
  • (B) has power to regulate commerce
  • (C) is revered by the people
  • (D) previously passed health laws
  • (E) raised insufficient revenues
Show answerHide answer

B · Institutional power

In this passage, commerce means trade. B restates the power explicitly attributed to Congress; the other choices add a judgment or a past event that is not established.

Evidence: Line 13 says “Congress, who alone can regulate trade.”

3. The word “visited” (line 3) most closely corresponds to

  • (A) called upon
  • (B) joined in
  • (C) traveled
  • (D) remained
  • (E) afflicted
Show answerHide answer

E · Vocabulary in context

The subject is a disease that has caused suffering. Here “visited” means afflicted, rather than a person travelling somewhere.

Evidence: In lines 1–3, the cities and towns have been “visited” by an alarming and destructive pestilence.
