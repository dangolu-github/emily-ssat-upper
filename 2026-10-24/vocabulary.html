Answer Chopeless

Show explanationHide explanation
  • Meaning / prediction: having lost hope; deeply discouraged.
  • Form: adjective describing a person's state of mind.
  • Nuance: looks forward and finds no hope; it is not about a past action.

Each option

  • AObsessive means unable to stop thinking about one thing; not about hope.
  • BAbominable means hateful or detestable; it judges a thing, not a mood.
  • CHopeless = without hope: match.
  • DRegretful is sorry about something already done; it looks backward, while despondent looks ahead and sees nothing.
  • EBold is close to the opposite: a despondent person has no spirit for daring.

Answer Buneasy

Show explanationHide explanation
  • Meaning / prediction: anxious about what may happen.
  • Form: adjective describing a feeling, not an action.
  • Nuance: a mild to moderate worry about the future, weaker than fear or terror; a feeling, not a way of behaving.

Each option

  • AConfident is the opposite.
  • BUneasy = mildly anxious: match in direction and in degree.
  • CTerrified is on the same scale but far too strong; the answer must keep the degree.
  • DCautious describes careful behaviour, which may come from apprehension but is not the feeling itself.
  • ETalkative concerns speech.

Analogies · Degree

Intensity: stronger and weaker

Choose the pair that shows the same relationship as the first pair.

  1. Name both meanings. Then write one specific connector sentence for the first pair.
  2. Keep the order. Read every option with the same connector in the same direction.
  3. Test all five. Same topic is not the same relationship; keep the pair whose sentence stays true.

Intensity and degree

  • What it is: the second word is a stronger, weaker, more extreme or more polite version of the first. A breeze is a weaker version of a gale. To guzzle is to drink to an extreme degree.
  • Connector sentence: ___ is a stronger (weaker) version of ___; for verbs, to ___ is to ___ to an extreme degree. Say it for the first pair, then for every option.
  • Direction: weak → strong (trot → gallop) or strong → weak (whirlpool → eddy). The answer keeps the direction of the first pair; a pair on the right scale in the wrong direction is out.
  • Same scale: the two words must lie on one scale: speed, size, force, politeness, feeling. A pair that merely sounds related (cyclone → water) is out.
  • Not the same as: synonym (candor : honesty, same strength), antonym (buy : sell), or part–whole (day : month).
  • How it meets tone questions: distrust < disdain; opposition < condemnation; cheerful < exuberant. Tone options are rungs on a ladder, and the right rung is the one the evidence words reach.

Words to know

  • Degree words: slight, mild, moderate, intense, extreme, utter; a lesser version of, a greater version of.
  • Scales in these items: trot → gallop; breeze → gale → hurricane; flurry → blizzard; squall / storm; seep → deluge; eddy → whirlpool; gust → tornado; sip → guzzle; hint → proclaim; request → demand; advise → order; admire → venerate; taste / sample.
  • Tricky words: gale (a very strong wind); deluge (a flood; a great downpour); seep (to flow slowly through small openings); maelstrom (a violent whirlpool); eddy (a small circular current of water or air); guzzle (to drink greedily and fast); venerate (to regard with deep respect, almost worship); proclaim (to announce openly and officially); portend (to be a sign that something, usually bad, will happen); prance (to move with high, springing steps); strut (to walk stiffly and proudly); squall (a sudden, short, violent wind or storm); parch (to dry out completely); quench (to satisfy thirst; to put out a fire); drench (to wet thoroughly).

Answer Ebreeze is to gale

Show explanationHide explanation
  • Connector: a trot is a weaker (slower) version of a gallop.
  • Direction: weak → strong; the answer must also run weak → strong.
  • Scale: speed of a horse's gait; the answer may use any scale, but must stay on one scale.

Each option

  • ATo prance and to strut are two showy ways of moving, equal in strength: a synonym pair, not a degree.
  • BTo drain removes water and a flood brings it: opposite directions, not two degrees of one thing.
  • CAn echo is a sound that comes back; a kind of sound, not a weaker version of sound.
  • DA squall is a sudden short storm: a kind of storm, and it can be violent, so it is not a fixed weaker rung.
  • EA breeze is a weaker version of a gale, on the wind scale, weak → strong: match.
