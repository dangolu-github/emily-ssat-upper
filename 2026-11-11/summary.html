Q1 · Keep three words apart

  • contempt (n.) · the feeling that someone or something is worthless or beneath you. Same family as disdain and scorn.
  • content (adj.) / contentment (n.) · happy and satisfied. Only the spelling is close to contempt.
  • indifference · not caring; no interest and no feeling. Words close to it: disinterest, unconcern, impassiveness (showing no feeling), and detachment in the sense of standing back from your feelings (its first meaning is simply separation).
  • Indifference is a noun here; indifferent is the adjective.

Q16 · How to test a degree pair

  1. Check the tone of both words first. portend = to be a sign that something is coming; warn = to tell someone about a danger, so it points to something bad. When the two words of an option do not match in tone, remove it in the first round.
  2. Then the degree. A hint gives a little; to proclaim is to announce it openly. Sip a little, guzzle a lot. Same action, more force: B.
  3. It is not a sequence. Hinting does not have to come before proclaiming; either can happen without the other.
  4. Do not build your own story for the pair (“first a sign, then a warning”). Compare the two words on one scale only.

Q19 · When you do not know the first word

  1. Parts of speech. The first word of every option is an adjective (patience is the noun, so D is already weak); the second is a person or a job. So the connector is: a person in this role is …
  2. Remove pairs with no link. E · brief (short) has nothing to do with a pundit, an expert who is often asked to give opinions, as in a political pundit. A · duplicitous (two-faced) is the opposite of what an ally should be.
  3. Test the tight sentence. “You must be X to be a Y.” A sage must be wise: C passes. A doctor need not be patient, and option B fails in the same way: B and D fail for the same reason, so neither can be the one answer.
  4. solicitous · caring and attentive about someone’s well-being. Not reclusive, which means wanting to live alone, away from people.

2. Two quick checks

RECURRENT · repeated

  • recurrent = happening again and again, with gaps in between. You answered this one correctly.
  • continuous = going on without a break. That is the trap.
  • current has two meanings: present, happening now, and a steady flow of water or air. Neither means repeating.

Stale : bread · rancid : meat

  • Connector: stale is what bread becomes when it goes old; rancid is what meat or fat becomes when it goes bad.
  • pulpy : juice fails: pulpy is a texture (bits of fruit in it), not juice that has gone bad.

How to guess when you do not know a word

  • Decide the part of speech of both words, then remove options that do not have the same pattern.
  • Concrete or abstract: money and words are things you can use up; concern and surprise are not. Remove the abstract ones.
  • Two options that pass or fail for the same reason cannot both be the answer: look for the one that is different.

Words from the options

  • boisterous · noisy and full of energy. It can be negative (a boisterous class) or positive (boisterous laughter). Not boastful, which means bragging.
  • heretic (n.) · a person who holds beliefs against the official teaching of a religion. Adjective: heretical.
  • orthodox · following the traditional, generally accepted beliefs. The opposite of heretical. It is also used outside religion: an orthodox method.
  • Deeply religious: devout, pious (Class Words · 07). Not religious, about everyday life: secular.

4. Reading: details and words in context

Both passages come from Charles Darwin’s On the Origin of Species (1859), a famous science classic.

Words in context: three steps

  1. Say your own word for the meaning before you look at the options.
  2. Test the everyday meaning against the sentence: who is doing it, and do they mean to?
  3. Read one sentence before and one after. The neighbours usually decide it.

Q8 · concurring (line 31)working together toward one resultTo concur usually means to agree; it can also mean to happen at the same time. Here neither fits: the checks are not people with opinions, and they act at different periods. They combine to decide the number.

Details: read the structure first

  • The topic sentence is a comparison. Organisms that produce eggs or seeds by the thousand vs those that produce extremely few. Find the two groups before you read on.
  • How the passage moves: the comparison → examples (condor and ostrich, the petrel, two flies) → the general explanation (most destruction comes early in life) → the tree example → the conclusion (numbers depend only indirectly on eggs or seeds).
  • Locate by the subject. Q1: find the sentence about the petrel; keep both facts (one egg; believed to be the most numerous bird).
  • Keep every condition. Q2: supposing that the seed is never destroyed and is sure to sprout in a fitting place → survived and was certain to sprout.
  • NOT / EXCEPT. Q3: mark each statement S (supported) or N. Look for general claims in the general part of the passage. Only indirectly is still an effect, so “has no effect” is the one that is not supported.
