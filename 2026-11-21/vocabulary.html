Leaving Home,
Finding Work

Fifteen words for why people leave home, what they find in the city and the work they do there.

1

Fifteen words: leaving, arriving, working

Leaving home, finding work: push, arrival, labor

  • Push and pull: people leave because something drives them out (penury, failed harvests, persecution) or draws them in (work, wages, family already there).
  • Words for what drives people: impetus (n.), impel and galvanize (v.) name a cause; ask what is being pushed, and into what.
  • Arrival: newcomers often settle together in an enclave, in a polyglot city, and meet welcome and xenophobia side by side.
  • Work: the first jobs are usually menial and badly paid; shared grievances are where protest and reform begin.
  • Value words carry a judgement: pittance, exorbitant, drudgery already say “unfair” or “too much”; a neutral word (payment, price, work) does not.

Say the meaning and one word it is not, before you open a card. Then read the sentence and say what in it proves the meaning.

Leaving: what drives people out

impetus

n.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

a force or reason that pushes something to start or to move faster

Two failed harvests were hard enough, but the real impetus for leaving was a letter from a cousin who had found steady work in Chicago.

Not the same as: an impulse (a sudden urge from inside you): an impetus usually comes from outside, from events.

impel

v.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

to drive or force someone to act

Debts she could never repay impelled the widow to sell the farm and take her four children to the mill towns.

Not the same as: repel (to push back or away) and expel (to force out of a place): to impel is to push someone into doing something.

exodus

n.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

a departure of a great many people at the same time

When the mine closed, the exodus from the valley emptied whole streets within a single summer.

Not the same as: a journey made by one family; an exodus is a mass departure, usually for good.

galvanize

v.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

to shock or stir people into sudden action

News of the fire, which had trapped workers behind locked doors, galvanized the city: within a week thousands marched for safety laws.

Not the same as: to frighten: a galvanized crowd does something.

penury

n.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

extreme poverty

Having arrived with nothing, the family lived in penury for two winters, sharing one room, one stove and one good coat.

Not the same as: penalty (a punishment) and penitence (regret for wrongdoing).

Arriving: newcomers in the city

influx

n.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

the arrival of large numbers of people or things

The influx of workers after the war doubled the town’s population and left landlords dividing every house into flats.

Not the same as: influence (the power to change someone). Influx is about numbers coming in.

enclave

n.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

a small area whose people differ in language or culture from those around them

In the enclave near the docks, shop signs, newspapers and sermons were all in Norwegian.

Not the same as: a suburb (a district on the edge of a city): an enclave is defined by who lives there, not by where it is.

polyglot

adj.; n.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

speaking or using several languages; a person who does

The polyglot crowd at the market bargained in Yiddish, Italian and a broken English that everyone somehow understood.

Not the same as: fluent (speaking one language well). A polyglot street has many languages at once.

xenophobia

n.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

fear or hatred of foreigners and strangers

Pamphlets that blamed newcomers for every lost job fed a xenophobia that later shaped the immigration laws.

Not the same as: dislike of one person: xenophobia is aimed at a whole group because it is foreign.

indigent

adj.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

very poor; lacking money for basic needs

The settlement house offered free meals and a clinic to indigent families on the block.

Not the same as: indigenous (native to a place) and indignant (resentful at unfair treatment).

Working: the jobs and their cost

drudgery

n.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

hard, dull, tiring work

Twelve hours of drudgery at the looms left the girls too tired to open the books they had saved for.

Not the same as: a challenge (hard work that holds your interest). Drudgery is hard and dull.

menial

adj.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

lowly and needing little skill (of work)

Though he had been a teacher in Naples, in New York he could find only menial work, scrubbing the floors of a hotel kitchen.

Not the same as: tedious (boring) or easy: menial work can be exhausting, even interesting; it is low in status, not in effort.

pittance

n.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

a very small and unfair amount of money

For sewing forty shirts a day she earned a pittance, barely enough for bread and rent.

Not the same as: a wage or payment in general: a pittance is always too little for what was done.

exorbitant

adj.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

far too high (of prices, rents or demands)

Landlords charged exorbitant rents for rooms with no window, knowing the newcomers had nowhere else to go.

Not the same as: expensive: a thing can be expensive and fair; exorbitant means unfairly high.

grievance

n.
Meaning & exampleHide meaning & example

a complaint about unfair treatment; the wrong that is complained of

The workers wrote out their grievances: unpaid overtime, locked exits and fines for talking at the machines.

Not the same as: grief (deep sadness). A grievance is a wrong someone wants put right.
